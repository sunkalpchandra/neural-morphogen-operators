\subsection{Masked spatial reconstruction across \MSSections{} sections}



\inputtable{tab_headline}


NRDO improves masked-region reconstruction over every baseline tested, on
\MSSections{} sections spanning four technologies (\MSRuns{} runs), and the
improvement survives the conservative unit of analysis
(Table~\ref{tab:headline}).

Two units are reported because they answer different questions. With the
\emph{section} as the unit every model sees identical held-out blocks, so a
paired test removes the between-section variance that otherwise dominates; all
\MSNBaselines{} baselines are beaten with Holm-corrected $p \le \MSMaxHolm$.
But twelve of these sections are serial sections of one brain, and treating them
as independent overstates the effective sample size. Collapsing to
\MSSpecimens{} independent \emph{specimens} costs most of that power and is the
defensible test: the separation still survives Holm--Bonferroni correction across the
family of \MSNBaselines{} baselines against \MSSpecNSig{} of them (\MSSpecSigModels{}), winning on at least
\MSSpecSigMinWins{} specimens at $p \le \MSSpecSigMaxHolm$. The tightest, \MSSpecTightModel{}, has a bootstrap 95\% CI of \MSSpecTightCI{}
excluding zero. Every margin is \SeedRatioLo--\SeedRatioHi$\times$ the
across-seed noise floor, the smallest being \SeedRatioLoModel{} --- the same
comparison that fails to resolve. It does not survive against \MSSpecGraphWorstModel{}
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). The grouping is conservative and does not drive
the result: counting the two Stereo-seq stages separately gives \MSSpecAltN{}
specimens and the same \MSSpecAltNSig{} significant comparisons.

\paragraph{The gain comes from continuity, not from message passing.}
Two controls localize the effect, both measured on this benchmark. A non-spatial
autoencoder sees no coordinates and reaches \MSAEPearson{} purely through the
inverse-distance read-out head shared by every graph baseline, so the gap between
it and a graph model isolates \emph{message passing}: across \MSNGraphModels{}
graph models that margin spans \MSWorstGraphOverAE{} to \MSBestGraphOverAE{}
Pearson $r$, against \MSNMOOverAE{} for NRDO --- an order of magnitude larger. An
implicit neural field, continuous by construction but carrying no dynamics, is
the weakest model tested (\MSWeakestPearson{}, a gap of $\MSMaxDelta$). Continuity
alone is therefore insufficient and message passing adds little over a good
read-out head; it is the \emph{operator} that carries the gain, which the
parameter-matched $T{=}0$ control confirms (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).


\paragraph{The advantage is where the observations are not.}
The continuity argument makes a point-by-point prediction, not just a
section-level one: a field is defined everywhere, a graph is not, so the gap
should widen with distance from the nearest observation. Splitting the held-out
locations into quartiles by that distance, NRDO is \emph{last} in the nearest
quartile (\DistNearNMO{} against \DistNearBest{}, rank
\DistNearRank{}) and first in the farthest, \DistQ1Um--\DistQ4Um\,$\mu$m out,
by \DistFarGainPct\% relative (\DistFarNMO{} against \DistFarBest{} for
\DistFarBestModel{}). Close to data every model interpolates and the operator
buys nothing; far from it, only the continuous formulation still has a value to
read.

\paragraph{Spatial autocorrelation.}
Predicted maps beside measured ones are in Appendix~\ref{app:empirical},
Fig.~\ref{fig:reconstruction}.
NRDO does not lead on every structural metric. On SSIM it is second, behind
\MSBestAnySSIMModel{} (\MSNMOSSIM{} against \MSBestAnySSIM{}), and it has by
some margin the largest error in Moran's $I$
(\MSNMOMoran{} against \MSBestAnyMoran{} for \MSBestAnyMoranModel{}, a deficit of
\MSMoranDelta{}): the predicted fields are markedly more spatially autocorrelated
than the measurements. On the primary Visium section, where the diagnostic can be
read directly, the predicted map has $I = \NMOMoranPred$ against \MoranTrue{}
measured. SSIM measures local contrast on a rasterized map and Moran's $I$
global autocorrelation on the point set, and the pattern is the expected
signature of a dissipative operator attenuating high spatial frequencies --- the
same diffusion-driven smoothing analyzed for graph neural diffusion
\citep{grand2021,gread2022}. Section~\ref{sec:limitations} discusses the remedy.

\subsection{Four checks on the benchmark}
\label{sec:numerics}

\paragraph{The numerical machinery is load-bearing.}
Replacing the exponential integrator with explicit Euler, and the spectral
Laplacian with a five-point stencil, answers whether it is necessary or merely
elegant. One configuration cannot establish that a scheme has \emph{no} step-size
restriction, so we sweep \CfgN{}: diffusion magnitudes over
\CfgDiffLo--\CfgDiffHi{} crossed with lattices \CfgLatLo--\CfgLatHi. The
exponential scheme is stable at every step tested in all of them
($\Delta t \ge \CfgOursMin$), while the alternatives' stable steps shrink by up
to $\CfgSpread\times$ across the same range --- a factor of $\CfgWorstRatio$
between the two at the stiffest setting. That is a property of the scheme rather
than of the configuration we happen to use, and it is what
Proposition~\ref{prop:contraction} predicts. Reaching a $10^{-2}$ tolerance takes
\NumStrangSteps{} steps against \NumEulerSteps{} (Appendix~\ref{app:empirical}).

\paragraph{Budget, density and smoothness.}
Training to convergence on the one section whose ordering is budget-dependent
reverses it in our favor (\ConvNMO{} against \ConvBest{},
\ConvWins{}/\ConvNPairs{} seeds), so the fixed budget is conservative rather than
flattering, and the non-spatial autoencoder still matches the graph models there.
Under multiplicative count noise and random location dropout all models degrade
comparably, at a single seed per point which does not support a ranking between
them; we report them for completeness rather than as a result
(Appendix~\ref{app:empirical}). Sampling density behaves as a field-based formulation predicts. Subsampling a
\DensBase-location base to \DensLevel{} of its locations changes density without
changing anatomy; NRDO retains \DensNMO\% of its full-density accuracy against
\DensBest\% for \DensBestModel{} and \DensWorst\% for the weakest, on both
seeds. This does not track overall accuracy: at full density NRDO ranks
\DensNMOFullRank{}rd here. It is not the most accurate model on this section, it
is the one that loses least as sampling thins --- the property continuity is
supposed to buy.
A spectral-matching term bounds the over-smoothing rather than removing it:
$I_{\mathrm{pred}}$ bottoms out at \SpecMinIPred{} against a measured
\SpecITrue{} at every weight and both forms, so the smoothness belongs to the
operator class rather than the objective, and we keep $\lambda = 0$.

\subsection{The reconstruction preserves spatial-domain structure}
\label{sec:biology}

Correlation does not establish that a reconstruction remains usable for the
analyses a biologist would run on it, so we cluster the \emph{predicted}
expression at held-out locations and score it against annotations shipped with
the public data, over \BioSections{} sections with reference labels
(Appendix~\ref{app:empirical}, Table~\ref{tab:biology}). NRDO retains
\BioARIRet{} of the adjusted Rand index obtainable from the measured data,
against \BioARIRetBest{} for the closest baseline. This evaluation uses one seed
per configuration, so we read the ordering as indicative rather than established
(Appendix~\ref{app:empirical}, Table~\ref{tab:samplesizes}).

Across \BioSections{} annotated sections NRDO separates from the neural field
but not from the graph baselines: against \MSBestBaseline{} the ARI-retention
margin is $\BioARIStagateD$ on \BioARIStagateWins{} sections
($d_z = \BioARIStagateDz$, $p = \BioARIStagateP$). An earlier version of this
work reported that comparison as significant on a smaller set of annotated
sections; it does not survive the additional ones, and we report the larger
sample. The defensible claim is therefore the negative one: the operator reaches
its reconstruction accuracy \emph{without} degrading the spatial-domain
structure a downstream analysis depends on, which a model that smoothed its way
to a good correlation would have lost. One section (\BioExcluded) is excluded
because its reference clustering has ARI $<\BioMinARI$, at which a retention
ratio is dominated by its denominator rather than by the reconstruction.

\subsection{Learned dynamics}


\paragraph{The recovered length scale is close to its own initialization.}
The fitted operator has a median diffusion length of \NullBaseline\,$\mu$m. An
earlier version of this work read that as the same order as measured morphogen
gradients \citep{kicheva2007kinetics,yu2009fgf8}; a control retracts it. Refitting
the identical architecture at the identical budget on \emph{coordinate-shuffled}
sections --- expression permuted across positions, destroying all spatial
structure --- recovers \NullShuffled\,$\mu$m at $r = \NullShuffledR$, against
\NullInit\,$\mu$m untrained. The shuffled fit moves the length by
\NullShuffledShift\% from initialization and real tissue by \NullDataShift\%.
Sweeping the splat bandwidth and the lattice leaves it unchanged in microns while
it moves fourfold in lattice cells, so neither is the mechanism
(Table~\ref{tab:difflen_null}). We therefore withdraw the correspondence: the
median is largely a configuration constant. What the data determine is the
\emph{spread} --- at initialization every channel carries an identical length
scale, and training differentiates them across \DiffLenLo--\DiffLenHi\,$\mu$m. The anisotropy is
used rather than merely available: constraining $\mD$ to be isotropic costs
\AblIsotropicDelta{} Pearson $r$.

The dispersion relation (Fig.~\ref{fig:dynamics}c) decreases monotonically in
$|\vk|$, so the fitted operator is dissipative and supports no Turing band. This
is what the training objective selects for: \eqref{eq:steady} rewards making the
\emph{observed} configuration stationary, whereas a Turing-unstable operator
would render the homogeneous state unstable so that structure grows
spontaneously. Recovering a pattern-forming regime would require observations
away from steady state, in line with Theorem~\ref{thm:identifiability}.

\subsection{Transfer across tissue, species and resolution}

Transfer is reported under the same held-out-block protocol as everything else.
An earlier version conditioned on a random half of the target and scored the
complement, which is not comparable: on the Xenium target the median distance
from a scored location to the nearest visible one is \SplitXenRandom\,$\mu$m
under a random split against \SplitXenBlock\,$\mu$m under blocks, so it admits
exactly the neighbor leakage the block protocol exists to prevent. Under that
protocol a zero-shot model appeared to beat models trained on the target; under
blocks it does not.

The corrected result is negative. Applied unchanged from adult mouse brain to
human breast carcinoma NRDO reaches \TissueZeroShot{} against a training-mean
floor of \TissueFloor{} and an in-domain oracle of \TissueOracle{}: not weak
transfer but none, and every baseline transfers better. Cross-resolution is
better but still poor (\ResZeroShot{} against an oracle of \ResOracle{} over
\ResSharedGenes{} shared genes). We attribute this to the mechanism that produces
the in-domain gain --- NRDO fits an operator whose fixed point is the source
tissue, and relaxing toward that attractor is inappropriate when the target has a
different architecture, whereas local message passing encodes less about the
source. Proposition~\ref{prop:nystrom} makes the distinction precise:
discretization invariance of the \emph{encoder} does not entail portability of
the \emph{fitted} operator.

\subsection{Counterfactual perturbation}
\label{sec:results4}

We test whether the latent field encodes pathway-specific structure with a
split-half design: inject the latent direction that maximally raises one random
half of a morphogen pathway, integrate the operator, and ask whether the
\emph{held-out} half responds preferentially against a permutation null over
matched gene sets. Across \BeadNPathways{} pathways the enrichment does not reach
significance (\BeadNSig{} significant; mean held-out rank \BeadMeanRank\% against
a 50\% chance level).

Theorem~\ref{thm:identifiability} predicts this: the perturbation displaces
$\vz$ off the observed manifold $\mathcal{R} = \vz^\star(\Omega)$, where a single
stationary snapshot places no constraint on $f_\theta$. We therefore read the
latent--pathway correlations as coarse anatomy that pathway scores also track,
not as a pathway-specific representation. The experiment does establish a spatial
scale --- the response decays to half its peak within \BeadWidth\,$\mu$m. A
second pre-specified probe against Perturb-seq \citep{norman2019perturbseq} was
underpowered ($n = \PSNPerturb$) and is inconclusive
(Appendix~\ref{app:empirical}).
