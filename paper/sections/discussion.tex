\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
% =====================================================================

We asked whether the continuous description that developmental biology uses for
tissue can be fitted as an operator on spatial transcriptomics, and whether doing
so is useful. On the evidence presented it is, within limits we can state
precisely.

Across \MSSections{} sections and four technologies, treating tissue as a field
rather than a graph improves prediction at unmeasured coordinates against every
baseline tested under paired tests over sections. At the specimen level --- the
conservative unit, and the one we regard as decisive --- the separation from the
continuous baselines holds on every independent specimen, while that from the
graph baselines does not resolve. Three controls localize what remains: a
parameter-matched ablation attributes the gain to the operator rather than to
capacity, a non-spatial baseline shows message passing contributes little even at
convergence, and an implicit neural field shows continuity alone is not
sufficient. On the section whose ordering is budget-dependent, training to
convergence reverses it in our favor. And where reconstruction accuracy cannot
separate NRDO from the strongest graph baseline, spatial-domain structure does.

We are equally explicit about what the evidence does not support.
Theorem~\ref{thm:identifiability} shows a stationary snapshot constrains the
reaction network only on a null set; consistent with it, the recovered length
scale is close to its initialization, the predicted fields are too smooth in a
way a spectral loss bounds rather than removes, and under a held-out-block
protocol the fitted operator does not transfer to a new tissue.

We therefore regard NRDO as learning latent continuous operators that summarize
spatial gene-expression organization within a section, not as recovering
morphogen dynamics. Three directions would change that: more independent
specimens, an architecture whose smoothness is not intrinsic, and spatial data
paired with temporal or interventional sampling.
