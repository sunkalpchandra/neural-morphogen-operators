\begin{abstract}
Spatial transcriptomics is almost universally modelled as a graph over measured
spots, an inductive bias that ties the learned function to one sampling lattice
and admits no notion of expression between nodes. Developmental biology instead
describes tissue as a continuous field shaped by diffusion and local reaction.
This work introduces \textbf{Neural Morphogen Operators} (NMO), which encode irregularly
sampled expression into a continuous latent field, evolve it under a learned
anisotropic reaction--diffusion PDE
$\partial_t \vz = \nabla\!\cdot\!(\mD_\theta \nabla \vz) + f_\theta(\vz)$, and
decode at arbitrary coordinates. Solving the diffusion half-step exactly in the
Fourier domain under Strang splitting removes the CFL constraint and renders
$\mD_\theta$ as a physical length scale ($\DiffLenMedian\,\mu$m median).
On \NSections{} sections from \NDatasets{} public datasets spanning four
technologies, NMO improves masked-region reconstruction over graph and
Gaussian-process baselines ($r=\NMOPearson$ vs.\ $\BestBaselinePearson$). Two
controls locate the effect: an exactly parameter-matched ablation shows the
$0.43\%$ of weights forming the operator account for $\MatchedNoDynamicsDelta$
Pearson $r$, while a non-spatial baseline shows graph message passing adds at
most \BestGraphOverAE{} over distance-weighted interpolation against
\NMOOverAE{} for the operator. Three further tests are negative or null: the
fitted operator is dissipative with no Turing instability at any finite
wavenumber, its fields are the most over-smoothed of any model tested, a
pre-specified split-half perturbation test does not reach significance, and it
transfers \emph{worse} than the baseline it beats in-domain
($\TissueZeroShotRatio$--$\ResZeroShotRatio\times$). The continuous-operator
view buys in-domain accuracy by specialising to a single tissue; its portability
and mechanistic readings remain unsupported.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================

Spatially resolved transcriptomics reports gene expression together with the
physical coordinates of each measurement \citep{stahl2016visium,chen2015merfish}.
Nearly all machine learning built on it---spatial domain identification,
clustering, imputation, denoising---represents a tissue section as a graph over
the measured locations and applies message passing
\citep{hu2021spagcn,dong2022stagate,palla2022squidpy}. This matches how the data
arrive, but it fixes a strong inductive bias: the tissue \emph{is} the sampling
lattice. Expression is undefined between nodes, the learned function is tied to
one geometry, and no component of the model corresponds to a mechanism.

Developmental biology describes the same tissue as a continuous field: morphogens
are produced, diffuse, decay and interact, and cells read the resulting landscape
\citep{turing1952,wolpert1969positional,gierer1972theory}. Reaction--diffusion
models of this form account for phenomena from digit patterning
\citep{sheth2012hox} to Nodal/Lefty signalling \citep{muller2013nodal}, with
measured gradient lengths of tens to hundreds of microns
\citep{kicheva2007kinetics,yu2009fgf8}. If tissue organisation is generated by a
continuous process, a model of that process---not of the lattice it was sampled
on---is the natural object to fit.

The proposition is tested directly. \textbf{Neural Morphogen Operators} (NMO)
encode irregularly sampled expression into a continuous latent field, evolve it
under a learned anisotropic reaction--diffusion PDE, and decode at arbitrary
coordinates (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). The question addressed is where this helps
and where it does not; the stronger question of whether the fitted operator
recovers biochemistry is deliberately \emph{not} posed, since a single snapshot
cannot support it---a claim made precise in Theorem~\ref{thm:identifiability}.

\paragraph{Contributions.} (i) A PDE-constrained neural operator for spatial
omics coupling a graph kernel-integral encoder, a spectral reaction--diffusion
operator and a coordinate-free decoder (Section~\ref{sec:method}). (ii) An
unconditionally stable, interpretable integrator: diffusion applied exactly in
the Fourier domain under Strang splitting, removing the CFL constraint and
rendering $\mD_\theta$ as a diffusion length in microns. (iii) A correct
formulation of the physics loss---because the integrator is exact the usual PDE
residual is vacuous, and the content-bearing constraint is a \emph{steady-state}
residual. (iv) Two controls that locate the effect: an exactly parameter-matched
$T{=}0$ ablation, and a non-spatial baseline showing how little graph message
passing contributes. (v) Four negative results reported as results---no Turing
instability, worst-in-class spatial autocorrelation, a failed split-half
perturbation test, and worse transfer than the baseline surpassed in-domain.

\section{Related Work}
% =====================================================================

\textbf{Neural operators} learn maps between function spaces, giving approximate
independence from the discretisation \citep{kovachki2023neuraloperator}: Fourier
neural operators parameterise the kernel spectrally \citep{li2021fno}, graph
neural operators integrate a kernel over neighbourhoods on irregular meshes
\citep{li2020gno}, DeepONet factorises into branch and trunk networks
\citep{lu2021deeponet}, and message-passing solvers learn the numerical stencil
itself \citep{brandstetter2022message}. The graph form is used for irregular sampling and the spectral form for the
dynamics, the latter rendering the diffusion solve exact; the integrator follows classical exponential
time-differencing and operator splitting \citep{cox2002etdrk,strang1968splitting}
rather than soft PDE-residual penalties \citep{raissi2019pinn} or neural ODEs
\citep{chen2018neuralode}.

\textbf{Machine learning for spatial transcriptomics} is dominated by graph
models built for domain identification and denoising rather than prediction at
unmeasured coordinates---SpaGCN \citep{hu2021spagcn}, STAGATE
\citep{dong2022stagate}, SpatialDE/SPARK
\citep{svensson2018spatialde,sun2020spark}, DestVI \citep{lopez2022destvi}---which
is why they are adapted here (Section~\ref{sec:setup}); Spateo \citep{qiu2024spateo} is
closest in spirit but targets 3-D reconstruction rather than a transferable
operator. \textbf{Pattern formation} supplies the vocabulary
\citep{turing1952,kondo2010reaction,green2015morphogen}; RNA velocity
\citep{bergen2020scvelo} and perturbation models
\citep{lotfollahi2019scgen,roohani2024gears} model dynamics or interventions but
carry no explicit spatial operator---the gap addressed here. Reaction--diffusion
formulations have also been used to analyse over-smoothing in graph networks
\citep{gread2022,grand2021}, a connection the present results bear on directly.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================

A section provides $N$ measurements $\{(\vp_i, \vg_i)\}_{i=1}^N$ with positions
$\vp_i \in \R^2$ (isotropically normalised to $[-1,1]^2$) and expression
$\vg_i \in \R^G$. A latent field $\vz : \R^2 \to \R^C$ is posited and the model factored as
encode--evolve--decode.

\paragraph{Spatial encoder.}
Irregular sampling is handled by kernel-integral layers on a $k$-NN graph
\citep{li2020gno}, $\vz_i \leftarrow \sigma( W \vz_i + |\mathcal{N}(i)|^{-1}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)} \kappa_\theta(\vp_j-\vp_i)\odot\vz_j )$, where
$\kappa_\theta$ maps a \emph{relative} displacement to a per-channel gain;
depending on geometry rather than node identity is what lets an encoder fitted to
one lattice be evaluated on another. Features are splatted onto a regular
$H\times W$ lattice (Nadaraya--Watson, learnable bandwidth), yielding a field and
an \emph{occupancy} map that distinguishes ``tissue with no signal'' from ``no
measurement here'' --- the distinction the masked-region task hinges on.
Spectral blocks \citep{li2021fno} then refine the field.

\paragraph{The morphogen operator.}
The field evolves under
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \vz}{\partial t}
  = \nabla \!\cdot\! \big(\mD_\theta \nabla \vz\big) + f_\theta(\vz),
\label{eq:pde}
\end{equation}
with a per-channel symmetric positive-definite tensor
$\mD_c = L_c L_c^\top + \varepsilon I$ ($L_c$ lower triangular, so definiteness is
structural rather than penalised) and a pointwise reaction network $f_\theta$
implemented as $1\times1$ convolutions with a $\tanh$-bounded output.

Equation~\eqref{eq:pde} is stiff---the diffusion operator has eigenvalues scaling
as $-|\vk|^2$, so an explicit scheme needs $\Delta t = O(h^2/\|\mD\|)$---but for
constant-coefficient anisotropic diffusion it is diagonal in the Fourier basis,
so evolution over $\tau$ has a closed form,
\begin{equation}
E_c(\tau) = \mathcal{F}^{-1}\,
   \exp\!\big(-\vk^\top \mD_c \vk\, \tau\big)\, \mathcal{F} .
\label{eq:expstep}
\end{equation}
Composing \eqref{eq:expstep} with a midpoint reaction update under Strang
splitting \citep{strang1968splitting} yields the one-step map
$S_{\Delta t} = e^{\frac{\Delta t}{2}\mathcal{A}}\circ R_{\Delta t}\circ
e^{\frac{\Delta t}{2}\mathcal{A}}$, which is second-order accurate
(Proposition~\ref{prop:order}). Two structural guarantees follow; both are proved
in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} and verified numerically in
Table~\ref{tab:theory}.

\begin{proposition}[Unconditional stability, no CFL condition]
\label{prop:contraction-main}
For every $\tau\ge0$ and every $\mD\in(\mathcal{S}_{++}^2)^C$,
$\|e^{\tau\mathcal{A}_{\mD}}u\|_{L^2}\le\|u\|_{L^2}$, with equality only for
spatially constant $u$. Moreover the spatial mean is exactly conserved,
$\int_\Omega e^{\tau\mathcal{A}_{\mD}}u = \int_\Omega u$.
\end{proposition}

Definiteness is structural rather than penalised
($\lambda_{\min}(\mD_c)\ge\varepsilon$, Proposition~\ref{prop:spd}), so
Proposition~\ref{prop:contraction-main} holds along the entire optimisation
trajectory: no step size couples to learned parameters and no projection is
required. Combined with $\|f_\theta\|_\infty\le\|g\|_\infty$ it yields a
uniform absorbing set for the zero-mean component of radius
$\Delta t\|g\|_\infty|\Omega|^{1/2}/(1-\rho)$,
$\rho=e^{-\Delta t\lambda_{\min}\pi^2}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:bounded});
rollouts remain bounded for $10^3$ steps empirically. Finally $\mD_c$ is
interpretable: its eigenvalues are squared diffusion lengths per unit
pseudo-time, convertible to microns via the stored coordinate scale.

\paragraph{Decoder.}
Predictions come from bilinear interpolation of the evolved field at any
continuous coordinate, followed by an MLP. \emph{The decoder receives no
positional input}: given coordinates it could memorise a
coordinate-to-expression map, rendering the dynamics ornamental and every
ablation uninterpretable.

\paragraph{Objectives, and what a physics loss can mean here.}
The objective combines reconstruction (MSE plus a per-gene Pearson term, since
the spatial pattern of each gene is the quantity of interest), a weak Dirichlet
smoothness prior, and three physics terms. The conventional physics-informed
penalty---the PDE residual along the model's own trajectory---is however vacuous
for an exactly integrated operator.

\begin{proposition}[The trajectory residual is an integrator diagnostic]
\label{prop:vacuity-main}
Let $R_{\Delta t}(\vz;\theta) := \Delta t^{-1}(S_{\Delta t}\vz-\vz) -
(\mathcal{A}_{\mD}\vz+f_\theta(\vz))$. Then
$\|R_{\Delta t}\| = \tfrac{\Delta t}{2}\|[\mathcal{A}_{\mD},\mathcal{F}_\theta]\vz\|
+ O(\Delta t^2)$, uniformly over bounded parameter sets, and $R_{\Delta t}$ is
independent of the observations.
\end{proposition}

Minimising $\|R_{\Delta t}\|^2$ therefore cannot fit the model to data---the data
do not appear---and merely biases $\theta$ towards operators whose diffusive and
reactive parts commute, with a gradient signal vanishing as $O(\Delta t)$
(Appendix~\ref{app:vacuity}; observed order $1.00$ in Table~\ref{tab:theory}).
The constraint that does carry content is that the \emph{measured configuration
be a near-stationary state} of the learned operator:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PDE}} = \big\| \nabla\!\cdot\!(\mD_\theta \nabla \vz_T)
  + f_\theta(\vz_T) \big\|^2 ,
\label{eq:steady}
\end{equation}
the correct formalisation of the quasi-steady-state assumption for adult tissue,
constraining $\mD_\theta$ and $f_\theta$ jointly. Mass, Jacobian-norm and splitting-consistency terms are also included; the
ablations of Section~\ref{sec:ablations} show all three to be inert at the
weights adopted here.

\paragraph{Interpretability: dispersion analysis.}
Linearising \eqref{eq:pde} about a homogeneous $\vz^*$ gives $\partial_t
\delta\vz_{\vk} = (\mJ - \vk^\top\!\mD\vk)\,\delta\vz_{\vk}$ with $\mJ =
\partial f_\theta/\partial\vz|_{\vz^*}$, so $\max\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{spec}(\mJ
- |\vk|^2\mD)$ against $|\vk|$ is the dispersion relation of the \emph{learned}
operator (maximised over orientations of $\vk$, since $\mD$ is anisotropic).
Positive growth at finite $|\vk|$ with decay at $\vk=0$ is the classical Turing
signature: a falsifiable test rather than a post-hoc visualisation.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}
% =====================================================================

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_datasets.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Processed spatial sections}, coloured by total counts per
  location. The five sections differ in assay, sampling density (2.7k Visium
  spots to 104k MERFISH cells), gene panel (248--2087) and physical extent
  (2--10\,mm), which is precisely what the transfer experiments vary. Full
  inventory in Appendix~\ref{app:datasets}.}
  \label{fig:datasets}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Data.} \NDatasets{} public datasets span four spatial technologies
plus one non-spatial perturbation screen (\NSections{} sections,
\TotalLocations{} locations; Fig.~\ref{fig:datasets}). Raw reads are never used: vendor count matrices are consumed directly and a
SHA256 manifest is recorded per artifact. One
QC policy is applied throughout, then library-size normalisation, log transform,
and HVG selection that \emph{force-includes} every detected morphogen-pathway
gene, so the interpretability analysis is not evaluated on a set selection
already biased. Coordinates are normalised \textbf{isotropically}: anisotropic
rescaling would distort the Laplacian and make a learned diffusion coefficient
meaningless.

\paragraph{Task and splits.} Masked spatial reconstruction: contiguous tissue
blocks are hidden from the encoder and every model predicts expression at those
coordinates. Random spot-level splits leak, since a held-out spot is trivially
predicted from its correlated neighbours, so whole contiguous blocks are held out
\citep{roberts2017crossvalidation} and standardisation statistics are computed on
the training split alone.

\paragraph{Baselines.} A non-spatial autoencoder (the floor), a GCN
\citep{kipf2017gcn}, SpaGCN-style and STAGATE-style models
\citep{hu2021spagcn,dong2022stagate}, a graph transformer
\citep{vaswani2017attention}, and an exact GP \citep{rasmussen2006gp}. The graph
methods were not designed to predict at unmeasured coordinates, so each receives
the same inverse-distance read-out head---the most favourable choice available.
These variants are marked \emph{-style}; no claim is made that they reproduce the
authors' published numbers on the authors' own tasks.
All models share one training loop, masks, optimiser and evaluation code.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Per-gene Pearson and Spearman across held-out locations,
RMSE, SSIM \citep{wang2004ssim} on rasterised maps, and absolute error in Moran's
$I$ \citep{moran1950}, the last included because correlation and RMSE both reward
regression towards a smooth mean. Mean $\pm$ s.d.\ over \NSeeds{} seeds.

\subsection{Masked spatial reconstruction}

\paragraph{Graph message passing barely beats plain interpolation.}
The most informative row is the one included as a floor. The non-spatial
autoencoder sees no coordinates at all and reaches \AEPearson{} purely through
the inverse-distance read-out head shared by every graph baseline, so the gap
between it and a graph model isolates what \emph{message passing} contributes.
That contribution is close to nothing: across \NGraphModels{} graph models the
margin spans \WorstGraphOverAE{} to \BestGraphOverAE{} Pearson $r$, and
\NGraphBelowAE{} is worse than using no spatial information at all, while NMO
gains \NMOOverAE{} --- roughly \NMOvsGraphRatio$\times$ the best graph model's
margin. On this task the standard graph treatment extracts remarkably little
beyond distance-weighted interpolation; the control merits routine reporting.

\paragraph{One metric disagrees, and it is the one added as a guard.}
NMO is best on Pearson, Spearman, RMSE \emph{and} SSIM (\NMOSSIM{} against
\BestAnySSIM{}), but \emph{worst} on Moran's $I$ error: \NMOMoran{} against
\BestAnyMoran{}, a gap of $+\MoranDelta$. SSIM measures local contrast on a
rasterised map while Moran's $I$ measures global autocorrelation on the point
set, and NMO's local structure is good while its fields are far too
autocorrelated overall ($I = \NMOMoranPred$ against a measured \MoranTrue{};
Fig.~\ref{fig:reconstruction}). The cause is the dissipative operator
(Fig.~\ref{fig:dynamics}c), which attenuates high spatial frequencies---helpful
inside a masked block, harmful to texture.

\subsection{Learned fields and dynamics}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig5_dynamics.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{What the fitted operator looks like.} (a) A latent channel
  relaxing under the learned dynamics. (b) Per-channel diffusion tensors as
  length-scale ellipses, showing the anisotropy is used. (c) Dispersion relation:
  growth is negative at every finite wavenumber, so the operator is dissipative
  and supports no Turing band. (d) Diffusion lengths across channels.}
  \label{fig:dynamics}
\end{figure}

The fitted operator has a median diffusion length of \DiffLenMedian{}\,$\mu$m
(10--90th pct.\ \DiffLenLo--\DiffLenHi), the same order as measured morphogen
gradients \citep{kicheva2007kinetics,yu2009fgf8} ---a consistency check rather than a measurement of physical transport.

\paragraph{The learned operator is not Turing-unstable.}
The dispersion relation (Fig.~\ref{fig:dynamics}c) decreases monotonically in
$|\vk|$: growth is weakly positive only for the uniform mode and strictly
negative at every finite wavenumber, so no band of spatial modes is
spontaneously amplified and no characteristic wavelength exists. Fitted operators
were Turing-unstable in \TuringFrac\% of runs. From a stationary snapshot the
model learns a \emph{dissipative} operator whose fixed point is the observed
tissue---exactly what \eqref{eq:steady} asks for. The absence of a Turing band
reflects the data available, not evidence against reaction--diffusion accounts of
the biology; detecting genuine pattern-forming instability would require
observing tissue away from steady state.

\subsection{Transfer across tissue, species and resolution}

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig4_transfer.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Transfer is where the operator fails.} Zero-shot performance
  against the in-domain oracle and the training-mean floor, for (a) mouse brain
  $\rightarrow$ human breast carcinoma and (b) Visium $\rightarrow$ single-cell
  Xenium. The STAGATE-style baseline that NMO beats in-domain transfers
  substantially better in both settings.}
  \label{fig:transfer}
\end{figure}

This is the clearest negative result in the paper. Trained on adult mouse brain
and applied unchanged to human breast carcinoma---a different species, organ and
spatial architecture---NMO attains \TissueZeroShot{} zero-shot against a
training-mean floor of \TissueFloor{} and an in-domain oracle of
\TissueOracle{}. It is above the floor, so something transfers. But
\TissueZeroShotBaselineName{} reaches \TissueZeroShotBaseline{} in the same
setting, \TissueZeroShotRatio$\times$ better than the model that beat it
in-domain (Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer}); refitting only the decoder does not close
the gap (\TissueFineTune{}). Cross-resolution repeats the pattern: an operator
fitted to 55\,$\mu$m Visium spots and evaluated on single-cell Xenium sampling of
the \emph{same organ} reaches \ResZeroShot{} against \ResZeroShotBaseline{}, again
$\ResZeroShotRatio\times$ worse (Appendix~\ref{app:resolution}). All Xenium panel
genes are present in the raw Visium matrix, so the source is rebuilt with the
panel force-included (\ResSharedGenes{} shared genes); the gap is not an artefact
of thin gene overlap.

The opposite was anticipated, since an operator defined on the field rather than
on a lattice should tolerate changes of geometry. The most plausible
account---advanced as a hypothesis rather than a demonstration---is that the
advantage and the failure share a cause. The in-domain gain is obtained by
fitting an operator whose fixed point \emph{is} the source tissue; relaxation
towards that attractor is actively wrong when the target has a different
architecture, whereas local message passing encodes less about the source and
degrades more gracefully. Appendix~\ref{app:discretisation} makes the underlying
distinction explicit: Proposition~\ref{prop:nystrom} establishes discretisation
invariance of the \emph{encoder}, which does not entail portability of the
\emph{fitted} operator, and Theorem~\ref{thm:identifiability} indicates why.

\subsection{Developmental forecasting}
On the Stereo-seq series---the only dataset here with a genuine temporal
axis---integrating the encoded E9.5 field forward improves the field-level match
to E10.5 from \DevNMOZero{} at $T{=}0$ to \DevNMOBest{} at
$T{=}\DevNMOBestHorizon$, against \DevPersistence{} for predicting no change.
The learned operator therefore carries genuine temporal information, with an
interior optimum in $T$. Consecutive MOSTA stages are distinct embryos, so the
comparison is at the level of the rasterised field after coarse registration
(Appendix~\ref{app:development}).

\subsection{Counterfactual perturbation}
\label{sec:results4}

The pathway interpretation is tested by a falsifiable split-half design: the
latent direction maximally raising one random half of a morphogen pathway is
injected, the operator integrated, and the response of the \emph{held-out} half
assessed against a permutation null over matched gene sets.

\textbf{It fails.} Across \BeadNPathways{} pathways and \BeadSeeds{} seeds,
\BeadNSig{} reach significance (smallest $p=\BeadMinP$) and the mean held-out
rank is \BeadMeanRank\% against a 50\% chance level---held-out pathway members
respond, if anything, slightly \emph{less} than random genes. This rules out the reading Fig.~\ref{fig:latent}b would otherwise invite: the
correlations are consistent with the field encoding coarse anatomy that pathway
scores also track. The outcome is moreover anticipated by
Corollary~\ref{cor:ident}, since the perturbation evaluates $f_\theta$ off the
observed manifold $\mathcal{R}$ where the data impose no constraint. A scale is
nonetheless established: the response decays to half its peak within
\BeadWidth\,$\mu$m, matching the diffusion lengths of $\mD_\theta$. It is the
\emph{specificity}, not the range, that is rejected. A second pre-specified
probe against Perturb-seq \citep{norman2019perturbseq} was underpowered
($n=\PSNPerturb$) and is inconclusive (Appendix~\ref{app:perturbseq}).

\section{Ablations}
\label{sec:ablations}
% =====================================================================

The decisive control is \emph{$-$ dynamics}, which sets the integration horizon
to zero so the operator is never applied. The sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:results}b,
Appendix~\ref{app:abl}) uses a reduced budget at which the full model has
\emph{not} converged (\AblFull{} against \MatchedFull{}); since undertraining
compresses every delta, the two decisive variants were re-run at the benchmark
budget (\MatchedSeeds{} seeds; Fig.~\ref{fig:results}c), giving
\MatchedNoDynamics{} ($\MatchedNoDynamicsDelta$) for $T{=}0$ and
\MatchedNoReaction{} ($\MatchedNoReactionDelta$) without the reaction. The
$T{=}0$ value falls below \emph{every} baseline of Fig.~\ref{fig:results}a,
including the non-spatial one.

The control is \emph{exactly} parameter-matched: the operator is 9{,}536 of the
model's 2{,}198{,}336 parameters ($0.43\%$), and $T{=}0$ leaves them allocated but
unused, so architecture, parameter count and optimiser budget are identical and
the gap cannot be capacity. That matters because NMO is larger than the graph
baselines, so part of its Table~\ref{tab:benchmark} margin could be capacity; the
$T{=}0$ control separates those explanations, and it is accordingly weighted above the
margin.

Two ablations are honestly \emph{null}: removing the biological regularisers
changes held-out $r$ by \AblNoBioRegDelta{} and removing the PDE constraint terms
by \AblNoPDEDelta{}, both within seed noise. At the weights adopted here these terms are
inert. The rows are retained rather than dropped, since a reader is entitled to know
that three of the five loss terms of Section~\ref{sec:method} do no measurable
predictive work, whatever they contribute to the operator's form. Constraining
$\mD$ to be isotropic costs \AblIsotropicDelta, indicating the anisotropy is used
rather than merely available.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{One snapshot cannot identify a dynamical system.} This is not merely a
practical caveat but a structural one. Writing
$\mathcal{R}:=\vz^\star(\Omega)\subset\R^C$ for the range of the observed
latent field, Theorem~\ref{thm:identifiability} states that for $C\ge3$ the
stationarity constraint determines $f_\theta$ only on $\mathcal{R}$, a set of
Hausdorff dimension at most $2$ and hence Lebesgue-null: the admissible set
contains $f_\theta+\varphi$ for every $\varphi$ vanishing on $\mathcal{R}$, an
infinite-dimensional family. Reported diffusion lengths therefore characterise
the \emph{fitted operator}, not morphogen transport, and any counterfactual that
displaces $\vz$ off $\mathcal{R}$---as the perturbation of
Section~\ref{sec:results4} does by construction---is fixed by the architecture
and regularisers rather than by the data (Corollary~\ref{cor:ident}). The null
outcome reported there is thus predicted by the theory, not merely observed.

\paragraph{Latent, not molecular; the name describes the operator class.}
Latent/pathway correlations show alignment, but the split-half test of Section~\ref{sec:results4} rejects
the stronger reading (Section~\ref{sec:results4}). ``Morphogen'' names the
\emph{operator class}---anisotropic reaction--diffusion---not a validated claim
about signalling. Two of three interpretability probes returned negative and a
third was underpowered; what survives is a diffusion tensor with a physical
length scale and a perturbation half-width consistent with it.

\paragraph{Over-smoothing, worse than the baselines'.} Predicted maps are far
more autocorrelated than measured ones ($\NMOMoranPred$ vs $\MoranTrue$): NMO is
\emph{worse} than every baseline on Moran's $I$ by $+\MoranDelta$ while leading
on SSIM by $\SSIMDelta$. The dissipative operator that delivers the accuracy gain
inflates the autocorrelation --- one phenomenon, not two. Predicting expression
at unmeasured coordinates is supported; delineating sharp boundaries, rare niches
or tumour margins is not.

\paragraph{The operator is tissue-specific.} Discretisation invariance motivated
preferring an operator to a graph, yet under transfer the graph baseline wins by
\TissueZeroShotRatio$\times$ and \ResZeroShotRatio$\times$; note that
Proposition~\ref{prop:nystrom} concerns the \emph{encoder} alone. Until a shared
operator is fitted across tissues, or $\mD_\theta$ conditioned on tissue
covariates, this remains a within-section model.

\paragraph{Adapted baselines and limited scope.} The \emph{-style} variants implement each method's architectural core with a
common read-out head: fair on \emph{this} task, but not a reproduction of
published results. The in-domain margin is a few points of Pearson $r$ on one
section per condition over few seeds, without a power analysis, and scope is
limited to single 2-D sections and CPU-scale compute.

% =====================================================================
\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
% =====================================================================

The question posed was whether the continuous description developmental biology
uses for tissue can be fitted as an operator on spatial transcriptomics. The
answer is a qualified affirmative with a sharp boundary. In-domain the
formulation helps: the parameter-matched $T{=}0$ control costs more than the
entire margin over the best baseline, and on the one dataset with a real time
axis integration beats persistence, while the exact spectral solve renders the
operator stable and physically readable.

Outside that boundary the results are negative, and they are the more useful half
of the paper: the operator does not transfer, it is dissipative rather than
pattern-forming, its fields are the most over-smoothed of any model tested, and a
falsifiable test of the pathway interpretation fails outright. The
continuous-operator framing buys in-domain accuracy by specialising to one
tissue; portability and mechanism remain unearned, and breaking the identifiability barrier of
Theorem~\ref{thm:identifiability} requires dense temporal sampling or
interventional spatial data.

\label{endofmain}

\section{Notation and function-space setting}
\label{app:notation}
% =====================================================================

Throughout, $\Omega = [-1,1]^2$ is identified with the flat torus
$\mathbb{T}^2 = (\R/2\Z)^2$, so that the spectral operators below are exact
rather than approximate. Let $L^2(\Omega;\R^C)$ carry the inner product
$\langle u,v\rangle = \int_\Omega u(x)^\top v(x)\,\dd x$ and norm
$\|u\| = \langle u,u\rangle^{1/2}$. The Fourier transform is
\begin{equation}
\hat{u}(\vk) = \tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_\Omega u(x)\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\vk\cdot x}\dd x,
\qquad
u(x) = \sum_{\vk\in\Lambda} \hat{u}(\vk)\,e^{\mathrm{i}\vk\cdot x},
\qquad
\Lambda = (\pi\Z)^2 ,
\label{eq:fourier}
\end{equation}
with Parseval identity $\|u\|^2 = |\Omega|\sum_{\vk\in\Lambda}|\hat u(\vk)|^2$.
The lowest non-zero wavenumber on $\Omega$ is $|\vk|_{\min} = \pi$.

Let $\mathcal{S}_{++}^2$ denote the cone of symmetric positive-definite
$2\times2$ matrices. A \emph{diffusion tensor field} is a tuple
$\mD = (\mD_1,\dots,\mD_C) \in (\mathcal{S}_{++}^2)^C$, acting channelwise. The
\emph{reaction field} is a map $f_\theta \in C^1(\R^C;\R^C)$ applied pointwise,
$(f_\theta u)(x) = f_\theta(u(x))$.

\begin{definition}[Morphogen operator]
\label{def:operator}
For $\mD \in (\mathcal{S}_{++}^2)^C$ and $f_\theta \in C^1(\R^C;\R^C)$ the
\emph{morphogen operator} is the semilinear evolution equation
\begin{equation}
\partial_t \vz = \mathcal{A}_{\mD}\,\vz + f_\theta(\vz),
\qquad
(\mathcal{A}_{\mD}\vz)_c := \nabla\!\cdot\!\big(\mD_c \nabla \vz_c\big),
\label{eq:operator}
\end{equation}
on $\Omega$ with periodic boundary conditions and initial datum
$\vz(\cdot,0)=\vz_0 \in L^2$.
\end{definition}

Equation~\eqref{eq:operator} is the classical semilinear parabolic form: a
strongly elliptic second-order generator plus a globally Lipschitz, bounded
perturbation. Existence and uniqueness of a mild solution on $[0,\infty)$ is
standard, and is recalled as Proposition~\ref{prop:wellposed} for completeness.

\section{Spectral representation of the anisotropic generator}
\label{app:spectral}
% =====================================================================

\begin{lemma}[Fourier symbol]
\label{lem:symbol}
For each channel $c$, $\mathcal{A}_{\mD_c}$ is diagonal in the basis
\eqref{eq:fourier} with symbol
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathcal{A}_{\mD_c} u}(\vk) \;=\; -\,q_c(\vk)\,\hat{u}(\vk),
\qquad
q_c(\vk) := \vk^\top \mD_c \vk \;\ge\; \lambda_{\min}(\mD_c)\,|\vk|^2 \;\ge\; 0 .
\label{eq:symbol}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Writing $\mD_c=(d_{ij})$ and $u = e^{\mathrm{i}\vk\cdot x}$,
$\nabla u = \mathrm{i}\vk\,u$, hence $\mD_c\nabla u = \mathrm{i}\,\mD_c\vk\,u$
and $\nabla\!\cdot\!(\mD_c \nabla u) = \mathrm{i}\vk \cdot (\mathrm{i}\mD_c\vk)u
= -(\vk^\top \mD_c\vk)\,u$. The inequality is the Rayleigh bound for symmetric
$\mD_c$.
\end{proof}

Consequently the diffusion semigroup admits the closed form used in the
implementation,
\begin{equation}
\big(e^{\tau \mathcal{A}_{\mD}}u\big)^{\wedge}(\vk) \;=\;
  e^{-q_c(\vk)\tau}\,\hat{u}_c(\vk),
\label{eq:semigroup}
\end{equation}
which is evaluated by a pair of FFTs at cost $O(CHW\log HW)$ per half-step. No
spatial discretisation of $\nabla\!\cdot\!(\mD\nabla\cdot)$ is ever formed, so the
diffusive update carries no truncation error beyond the spectral representation
of $\vz$ itself.

\begin{remark}[Why anisotropy is retained]
Isotropic $\mD_c = d_c I$ gives $q_c(\vk)=d_c|\vk|^2$, a symbol invariant under
rotations of $\vk$. The general case has level sets that are ellipses with
principal axes given by the eigenvectors of $\mD_c$, which is what permits
different decay lengths along and across an anatomical axis. The ablation of
Section~\ref{sec:ablations} measures whether this freedom is used.
\end{remark}

\section{Structural guarantees: proofs}
\label{app:proofs}
% =====================================================================

\begin{proposition}[Structural positive-definiteness]
\label{prop:spd}
Let $L_c$ be lower triangular with $\mathrm{diag}(L_c)=\mathrm{softplus}(\ell_c)$
and let $\mD_c := L_cL_c^\top + \varepsilon I$ with $\varepsilon>0$. Then
$\mD_c \in \mathcal{S}_{++}^2$ with $\lambda_{\min}(\mD_c)\ge\varepsilon$, for
every value of the unconstrained parameters.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$L_cL_c^\top$ is symmetric positive semi-definite, so for any $v\ne0$,
$v^\top \mD_c v = \|L_c^\top v\|^2 + \varepsilon\|v\|^2 \ge \varepsilon\|v\|^2$.
Symmetry is immediate. Hence the spectrum lies in $[\varepsilon,\infty)$.
\end{proof}

The significance is that definiteness is a property of the \emph{parametrisation}
rather than of the optimiser's trajectory: no penalty enforces it and no
projection step is required, so it cannot be violated transiently during
training.

\begin{proposition}[Unconditional $L^2$ contraction]
\label{prop:contraction}
For every $\tau \ge 0$ and every $\mD \in (\mathcal{S}_{++}^2)^C$,
$\|e^{\tau\mathcal{A}_{\mD}}u\| \le \|u\|$, with equality iff $u$ is
spatially constant. In particular the diffusive half-step imposes no
Courant--Friedrichs--Lewy restriction on $\Delta t$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Parseval and \eqref{eq:semigroup},
$\|e^{\tau\mathcal{A}}u\|^2 = |\Omega|\sum_{\vk,c} e^{-2q_c(\vk)\tau}
|\hat u_c(\vk)|^2 \le |\Omega|\sum_{\vk,c}|\hat u_c(\vk)|^2 = \|u\|^2$, since
$q_c(\vk)\ge0$ gives $e^{-2q_c\tau}\in(0,1]$. Equality forces
$e^{-2q_c(\vk)\tau}=1$ wherever $\hat u_c(\vk)\ne0$; for $\tau>0$ this requires
$q_c(\vk)=0$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:symbol} with $\lambda_{\min}\ge\varepsilon>0$
this holds only at $\vk=0$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:contraction} is the formal content of the stability claim.
An explicit scheme applied to \eqref{eq:operator} is stable only for
$\Delta t \lesssim h^2/\lambda_{\max}(\mD)$, a constraint that couples the step
size to \emph{learned} parameters and can therefore be violated mid-training as
$\mD_\theta$ adapts. The exponential update removes the constraint entirely.

\begin{proposition}[Exact conservation of the mean]
\label{prop:mass}
$\int_\Omega (e^{\tau\mathcal{A}_{\mD}}u)_c \,\dd x = \int_\Omega u_c\,\dd x$
for all $\tau\ge0$ and all $c$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The integral equals $|\Omega|\hat u_c(0)$, and by \eqref{eq:semigroup} the mode
$\vk=0$ is multiplied by $e^{-q_c(0)\tau}=e^0=1$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:mass} licenses the interpretation of the mass penalty in
Section~\ref{sec:method}: because the diffusive half-step is exactly
conservative, any drift in $\int\vz$ along a trajectory is attributable to
$f_\theta$ alone, and penalising $\int_\Omega f_\theta(\vz)$ is therefore a
statement about the reaction network rather than a numerical artefact.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform bound on the reaction]
\label{prop:reaction}
Let $f_\theta(u) = g \odot \tanh(h_\theta(u))$ with $g\in\R_{>0}^C$. Then
$\|f_\theta(u)\|_\infty \le \|g\|_\infty$ for all $u$, and $f_\theta$ is globally
Lipschitz with constant $L_f = \|g\|_\infty \,\mathrm{Lip}(h_\theta)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$|\tanh|\le1$ channelwise gives the first claim. For the second,
$\|\nabla_u f_\theta\| = \|\mathrm{diag}(g)\,\mathrm{diag}(1-\tanh^2)\,
\nabla_u h_\theta\| \le \|g\|_\infty \|\nabla_u h_\theta\|$ since
$0<1-\tanh^2\le1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Well-posedness]
\label{prop:wellposed}
Under Proposition~\ref{prop:reaction}, \eqref{eq:operator} admits a unique mild
solution $\vz\in C([0,\infty);L^2)$ for every $\vz_0\in L^2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$\mathcal{A}_{\mD}$ generates an analytic contraction semigroup by
Proposition~\ref{prop:contraction}, and $f_\theta$ is globally Lipschitz and
bounded on $L^2$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:reaction}. The variation-of-constants
map $\vz\mapsto e^{t\mathcal{A}}\vz_0+\int_0^t e^{(t-s)\mathcal{A}}f_\theta(\vz(s))\dd s$
is a contraction on $C([0,T];L^2)$ for $T$ small, and the bound of
Proposition~\ref{prop:reaction} precludes finite-time blow-up, so the local
solution extends globally.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Second-order accuracy of the split step]
\label{prop:order}
Let $S_{\Delta t} := e^{\frac{\Delta t}{2}\mathcal{A}}\circ R_{\Delta t}\circ
e^{\frac{\Delta t}{2}\mathcal{A}}$, where $R_{\Delta t}$ is the explicit midpoint
update for $\dot{\vz}=f_\theta(\vz)$. For $\vz_0$ sufficiently regular the local
error is $O(\Delta t^3)$ and the error after $n=T/\Delta t$ steps is
$O(\Delta t^2)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Strang splitting of $e^{\Delta t(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{F})}$ has local error
$\tfrac{\Delta t^3}{24}\big([\mathcal{A},[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{F}]]
- 2[\mathcal{F},[\mathcal{F},\mathcal{A}]]\big)+O(\Delta t^5)$ by the
Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff expansion, and the midpoint rule contributes a further
$O(\Delta t^3)$ local error. Summation over $n$ steps with the stability of
Proposition~\ref{prop:contraction} gives the global rate.
\end{proof}

The predicted rate is confirmed numerically in Table~\ref{tab:theory}, which
reports an observed order of $2.00$ over a four-fold refinement.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform bound on the fluctuation component]
\label{prop:bounded}
Write $\vz = \bar{\vz} + \vz'$ where $\bar{\vz}$ is the spatial mean and
$\vz'$ has zero mean. Let $\rho := e^{-\Delta t\,\lambda_{\min}(\mD)\pi^2}<1$ and
$L := \Delta t\,\|g\|_\infty |\Omega|^{1/2}$. Then the iterates
$\vz_{n+1}=S_{\Delta t}\vz_n$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
\|\vz_n'\| \;\le\; \rho^{\,n}\|\vz_0'\| + \frac{L}{1-\rho}
\;\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\; \frac{L}{1-\rho},
\label{eq:bounded}
\end{equation}
and $\|\bar{\vz}_n\| \le \|\bar{\vz}_0\| + nL$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the zero-mean subspace every active wavenumber satisfies $|\vk|\ge\pi$, so by
Lemma~\ref{lem:symbol} the two half-steps contract $\vz'$ by at least
$e^{-\Delta t \lambda_{\min}\pi^2}=\rho$ in total. The reaction step adds at most
$\Delta t\|f_\theta\|\le L$ in $L^2$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:reaction}. Hence
$\|\vz_{n+1}'\|\le\rho\|\vz_n'\|+L$, and \eqref{eq:bounded} follows by summing the
geometric series. The mean is untouched by diffusion
(Proposition~\ref{prop:mass}) and receives at most $L$ per step.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:bounded} is stronger than mere absence of blow-up: the
non-constant part of the state possesses a bounded absorbing set whose radius is
independent of $n$ and of the initial condition. Only the spatial mean can drift,
and at most linearly---which is exactly the mode the mass penalty controls.

\section{Vacuity of the along-trajectory residual}
\label{app:vacuity}
% =====================================================================

Physics-informed training conventionally penalises the PDE residual evaluated on
the model's own trajectory. The following makes precise why that penalty is
uninformative for the present architecture, and motivates the steady-state
formulation adopted instead.

\begin{proposition}[The trajectory residual is an integrator diagnostic]
\label{prop:vacuity}
Define $R_{\Delta t}(\vz;\theta) := \Delta t^{-1}\big(S_{\Delta t}\vz - \vz\big)
- \big(\mathcal{A}_{\mD}\vz + f_\theta(\vz)\big)$. Then for every
$\theta$ in a bounded parameter set and every sufficiently regular $\vz$,
\begin{equation}
\|R_{\Delta t}(\vz;\theta)\| \;=\; \Delta t \cdot
\Big\| \tfrac{1}{2}\big[\mathcal{A}_{\mD},\,\mathcal{F}_\theta\big]\vz \Big\|
\;+\; O(\Delta t^2),
\label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{F}_\theta \vz := f_\theta(\vz)$ and $[\cdot,\cdot]$ is the
commutator. In particular $R_{\Delta t}\to0$ as $\Delta t\to0$ \emph{uniformly in
$\theta$}, and $R_{\Delta t}$ does not depend on the observed data.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Expanding $S_{\Delta t}$ by BCH and subtracting the exact generator leaves the
leading commutator term at order $\Delta t$; all data-dependent quantities cancel
because $S_{\Delta t}$ and the generator are evaluated at the same state $\vz$.
\end{proof}

Two consequences follow. First, minimising
$\|R_{\Delta t}\|^2$ cannot fit the model to observations, since the observations
do not appear in \eqref{eq:residual}; it drives $\theta$ towards operators whose
diffusive and reactive parts commute, an inductive bias unrelated to the data.
Second, the gradient signal it supplies is $O(\Delta t)$ and therefore vanishes
in the very limit in which the discretisation is most faithful. The empirical
scaling is confirmed in Table~\ref{tab:theory} (observed order $1.00$).

The constraint retained instead is the \emph{steady-state} residual
\eqref{eq:steady}, which asks that the measured configuration be a fixed point of
the learned operator. This does involve the data, through $\vz_T$, and it
constrains $\mD_\theta$ and $f_\theta$ jointly: the set of pairs satisfying it is
a proper subset of parameter space, characterised next.

\section{Non-identifiability from a single snapshot}
\label{app:identifiability}
% =====================================================================

The following formalises the central epistemic limitation of the setting: an
operator fitted to one stationary field is determined only on a measure-zero
subset of its own domain.

\begin{theorem}[Latent-space non-identifiability]
\label{thm:identifiability}
Let $\vz^\star \in C^2(\Omega;\R^C)$ with $\Omega\subset\R^2$ open, and suppose
$C \ge 3$. Fix any $\mD\in(\mathcal{S}_{++}^2)^C$ and define the admissible set
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}(\mD,\vz^\star) := \Big\{\, f\in C^1(\R^C;\R^C) \;:\;
  \mathcal{A}_{\mD}\vz^\star(x) + f\big(\vz^\star(x)\big)=0
  \;\;\forall x\in\Omega \,\Big\}.
\end{equation}
If $\mathcal{F}(\mD,\vz^\star)\ne\emptyset$ then it is an infinite-dimensional
affine subset of $C^1(\R^C;\R^C)$. Specifically, for every
$\varphi\in C^1(\R^C;\R^C)$ vanishing on $\mathcal{R}:=\vz^\star(\Omega)$ and
every $f\in\mathcal{F}$, one has $f+\varphi\in\mathcal{F}$, and the space of such
$\varphi$ is infinite-dimensional.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The constraint restricts $f$ only through its values on
$\mathcal{R}=\vz^\star(\Omega)$; hence $f+\varphi$ satisfies it whenever
$\varphi|_{\mathcal{R}}\equiv0$. It remains to show that the space of such
$\varphi$ is infinite-dimensional. Since $\vz^\star$ is $C^1$ on a
$2$-dimensional domain, $\mathcal{R}$ is a countable union of Lipschitz images of
compact subsets of $\R^2$ and therefore has Hausdorff dimension at most $2$. As
$C\ge3$, $\dim_H(\mathcal{R}) \le 2 < C$, so $\mathcal{R}$ is Lebesgue-null in
$\R^C$ and has empty interior. Its complement therefore contains an open ball
$B$; choosing countably many disjoint balls $B_i\subset B$ and bump functions
$\varphi_i\in C_c^\infty(B_i;\R^C)$ yields a linearly independent family, all
vanishing on $\mathcal{R}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[What a stationarity fit can determine]
\label{cor:ident}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:identifiability}, the steady-state loss
\eqref{eq:steady} constrains $f_\theta$ only on $\mathcal{R}$, a set of Lebesgue
measure zero in $\R^C$. Any statement about $f_\theta$ off $\mathcal{R}$---in
particular any counterfactual obtained by displacing the latent state away from
the observed manifold---is determined by the inductive bias of the architecture
and the regularisers, not by the data.
\end{corollary}

Corollary~\ref{cor:ident} has a direct experimental consequence. The in-silico
perturbation of Section~\ref{sec:results4} displaces $\vz$ along a latent
direction and integrates forward; by construction this evaluates $f_\theta$ at
states off $\mathcal{R}$, where Theorem~\ref{thm:identifiability} shows the data
exert no constraint. The null outcome reported there is therefore consistent
with the theory rather than merely an empirical disappointment: a single
stationary snapshot does not contain the information such a counterfactual would
require. Breaking the degeneracy requires observations that visit a
$C$-dimensional neighbourhood of $\mathcal{R}$---dense temporal sampling, or
interventional data.

\section{Linear stability and Turing analysis}
\label{app:turing}
% =====================================================================

Let $\vz^\star\in\R^C$ be a spatially homogeneous equilibrium, so
$f_\theta(\vz^\star)=0$, and write $\mJ := \nabla f_\theta(\vz^\star)$.
Linearising \eqref{eq:operator} about $\vz^\star$ and expanding the perturbation
in the basis \eqref{eq:fourier} gives, for each $\vk$, the finite-dimensional
system
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\,\delta\hat{\vz}(\vk)
  \;=\; M(\vk)\,\delta\hat{\vz}(\vk),
\qquad
M(\vk) \;:=\; \mJ - \mathrm{diag}\big(q_1(\vk),\dots,q_C(\vk)\big),
\label{eq:linearised}
\end{equation}
with $q_c$ as in \eqref{eq:symbol}. Define the growth rate
$\mu(\vk) := \max\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{spec}\,M(\vk)$ and its angular maximum
$\mu^\star(k) := \max_{|\vk|=k}\mu(\vk)$; the latter is required because
anisotropy makes $q_c$ direction-dependent.

\begin{definition}[Turing instability]
\label{def:turing}
The operator exhibits a \emph{diffusion-driven (Turing) instability} at
$\vz^\star$ if $\mu^\star(0)<0$ and $\mu^\star(k)>0$ for some $k>0$.
\end{definition}

The first condition states that the reaction alone is stable; the second that
diffusion destabilises a band of finite wavelengths. The selected wavelength is
$\lambda^\star = 2\pi/k^\star$ with $k^\star = \arg\max_k \mu^\star(k)$,
converted to microns by the stored coordinate scale.

\begin{proposition}[Pure diffusion cannot pattern]
\label{prop:nopattern}
If $f_\theta\equiv0$ then $\mu^\star(k) = -\min_c \min_{|\vk|=k} q_c(\vk) \le
-\lambda_{\min}(\mD)k^2 \le 0$, strictly decreasing in $k$. No Turing
instability exists.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$M(\vk)=-\mathrm{diag}(q_c(\vk))$ is diagonal with real non-positive entries;
its largest eigenvalue is $-\min_c q_c(\vk)$, and $q_c(\vk)\ge\lambda_{\min}|\vk|^2$
by Lemma~\ref{lem:symbol}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Two-channel criterion]
\label{prop:twochannel}
Let $C=2$ with isotropic tensors $\mD_c = d_c I$, $\mJ = \begin{psmallmatrix}
a & b \\ c & d\end{psmallmatrix}$, and write $\sigma=|\vk|^2$. Then
$\mu(\vk)>0$ for some $\sigma>0$ while $\mu(0)<0$ if and only if
\begin{equation}
a+d<0, \qquad ad-bc>0, \qquad d_2 a + d_1 d > 2\sqrt{d_1 d_2 (ad-bc)} .
\label{eq:turingcond}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$M$ has trace $a+d-(d_1+d_2)\sigma$ and determinant
$h(\sigma) := d_1d_2\sigma^2 - (d_2a+d_1d)\sigma + (ad-bc)$. The first two
conditions give stability at $\sigma=0$; the trace is then negative for all
$\sigma>0$, so instability requires $h(\sigma)<0$ for some $\sigma>0$. The
minimum of the convex parabola $h$ is attained at
$\sigma^\star=(d_2a+d_1d)/(2d_1d_2)$ and is negative precisely under the third
condition.
\end{proof}

Condition~\eqref{eq:turingcond} requires the two channels to have
\emph{unequal} diffusivities of opposite-signed feedback---the classical
short-range-activation / long-range-inhibition requirement. The operators fitted
in Section~\ref{sec:results} satisfy the first two conditions but not the third:
the dispersion curve is monotonically decreasing
(Fig.~\ref{fig:dynamics}c), and $\mu^\star(k)<0$ for all $k>0$ in every run.

\begin{remark}[Why the negative result is expected]
The training objective \eqref{eq:steady} rewards making the \emph{observed}
configuration stationary. A Turing-unstable operator does the opposite: it
renders the homogeneous state unstable so that structure grows spontaneously.
Since the observed field is treated as a fixed point rather than as the outcome
of an instability, the fitted operator is driven towards dissipativity. Detecting
pattern-forming instability would require observations away from steady state,
consistent with Corollary~\ref{cor:ident}.
\end{remark}

\section{Discretisation invariance of the encoder}
\label{app:discretisation}
% =====================================================================

The encoder must map measurements on an arbitrary point set to a field. Let
$\mu$ be the sampling measure on $\Omega$ and consider the kernel-integral
operator
\begin{equation}
(\mathcal{K}v)(x) \;=\; \int_\Omega \kappa_\theta(y-x)\odot v(y)\,\dd\mu(y),
\label{eq:kernelint}
\end{equation}
of which the graph layer is the empirical counterpart
$(\mathcal{K}_N v)(x_i) = |\mathcal{N}(i)|^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}
\kappa_\theta(x_j-x_i)\odot v(x_j)$.

\begin{proposition}[Monte-Carlo consistency]
\label{prop:nystrom}
If $\{x_j\}_{j=1}^N$ are i.i.d.\ draws from $\mu$ and
$\kappa_\theta\odot v \in L^2(\mu)$, then for each $x$,
$\E\big[(\mathcal{K}_Nv)(x)\big] = (\mathcal{K}v)(x)$ and
$\mathrm{Var}\big[(\mathcal{K}_Nv)(x)\big] = O(N^{-1})$; consequently
$\|\mathcal{K}_N v - \mathcal{K}v\|_{L^2(\mu)} = O_P(N^{-1/2})$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The empirical operator is a sample mean of i.i.d.\ terms with finite second
moment; unbiasedness and the $O(N^{-1})$ variance are immediate, and the norm
bound follows by Chebyshev and Fubini.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:nystrom} is the sense in which the encoder is
discretisation-invariant: the population operator \eqref{eq:kernelint} depends on
the geometry through $\kappa_\theta(y-x)$ alone and not on the sampling pattern,
and the empirical operator converges to it as the sampling density increases,
whatever the lattice. \textbf{This is an asymptotic statement about the encoder,
and it is worth emphasising that it does not imply transfer of the fitted
operator.} The transfer experiments of
Section~\ref{sec:results} show empirically that it does not: $\mD_\theta$ and
$f_\theta$ are fitted to one tissue's stationary field, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:identifiability} indicates they are unconstrained away from that
field's range. Discretisation invariance of the \emph{architecture} and
portability of the \emph{fit} are distinct properties, and only the former is
established here.

\begin{proposition}[Splatting consistency]
\label{prop:splat}
Let $w_\sigma(r)=e^{-r^2/2\sigma^2}$ and define the Nadaraya--Watson field
$F_\sigma(u) = \sum_i w_\sigma(\|u-x_i\|)v_i / \sum_i w_\sigma(\|u-x_i\|)$. If
$v_i = v(x_i)$ for $v\in C^2$ and the sampling density $p$ is bounded away from
zero, then $F_\sigma(u) = v(u) + O(\sigma^2) + O_P((N\sigma^2)^{-1/2})$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Standard kernel-regression bias--variance decomposition in two dimensions: the
bias term is $\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\big(\Delta v + 2\nabla v\cdot\nabla\log p\big)
+o(\sigma^2)$ and the variance is $O((N\sigma^2)^{-1})$.
\end{proof}

The learnable bandwidth $\sigma$ therefore trades a smoothing bias against
sampling noise. Since the bias term is proportional to $\Delta v$, splatting
contributes a further diffusive smoothing on top of the operator---a mechanism
that plausibly compounds the over-smoothing reported in
Section~\ref{sec:results}, and one that the present work does not disentangle.

\section{Complexity}
\label{app:complexity}
% =====================================================================

Let $N$ be the number of measured locations, $G$ genes, $C$ latent channels,
$H\times W$ the lattice, $E$ the number of graph edges and $T$ the integration
horizon. The costs per forward pass are: gene projection $O(NGd)$; graph layers
$O(LEd)$; splatting $O(Nr^2C)$ for radius $r$; spectral blocks
$O(BCHW\log HW)$; operator $O(TCHW\log HW)$; decoding $O(NCd + NGd)$. The
operator term is therefore linear in the horizon and quasi-linear in the lattice,
and for the configurations used here ($C{=}32$, $H{=}W{=}64$, $T{=}8$) it is
under $4\%$ of the forward cost---consistent with the parameter count of
Section~\ref{sec:ablations}, where the operator holds $0.43\%$ of the weights yet
accounts for the entire margin over the strongest baseline.

\section{Developmental forecasting: full results}
\label{app:development}

\inputtable{tab_development}

Every experiment so far fits a \emph{stationary} operator, because the data are
snapshots. The Stereo-seq developmental series is the single dataset here with a
real time axis, so it is the only setting in which the operator can act as a
forward-time predictor. The E9.5 field is encoded, integrated for $T$ operator steps, and the decoded
prediction scored against the measured E10.5 field
(Table~\ref{tab:development}).

The comparison must be read carefully. \textbf{Consecutive MOSTA stages are
different embryos}, not the same embryo imaged twice, so no cell-to-cell
correspondence exists and none can be manufactured by registration. Evaluation is therefore performed at the level of the rasterised field after
isotropic normalisation to a common frame, and the score inherits the error of
that coarse alignment. The
reference to beat is \emph{persistence} --- predicting E10.5 $=$ E9.5. If the
learned dynamics carried no temporal information, NMO could not exceed it.

Persistence attains field-level $r = \DevPersistence$, which is itself low and
quantifies how much the embryo changes (and how crude the registration is)
between these stages. Sweeping the horizon rather than tuning it, NMO reaches
\DevNMOBest{} at $T = \DevNMOBestHorizon$. The full sweep is reported so that the horizon is a stated quantity rather than a
hyperparameter selected against the test field. This experiment carries the least statistical power of any reported here and is
offered as an existence check on forward-time behaviour rather than as a
forecasting benchmark.


\section{Counterfactual perturbation: full protocol and results}
\label{app:pert}

The computational analogue of a morphogen bead implant is performed: inject the
latent direction that maximally raises a pathway's decoded activity into a small
disc, integrate the operator, and read out the spatial response. The design is
deliberately falsifiable and split-half---the direction is defined from a random
half of each pathway's genes and enrichment is scored on the \emph{held-out}
half---with a permutation null over matched random gene sets.

\paragraph{This test fails, and that is the primary outcome of the experiment.}
Across \BeadNPathways{} pathways and \BeadSeeds{} seeds, \BeadNSig{} reach
significance; the smallest per-pathway $p$ is \BeadMinP{} and the mean held-out
rank is \BeadMeanRank\% against a 50\% chance level, so if anything held-out
pathway members respond \emph{less} than random genes (Appendix~\ref{app:bead}).

The result rules out a reading the correlations in Fig.~\ref{fig:latent}b would
otherwise invite. Moving the latent direction that most raises one half of a
pathway does not preferentially move the other half, so those correlations are
consistent with the field encoding coarse anatomy that pathway scores also track,
rather than a pathway-specific representation. No claim is therefore made that NMO
recovers signalling programmes. The experiment does establish a scale: the
response decays to half its peak within \BeadWidth\,$\mu$m, matching the
diffusion lengths read off $\mD_\theta$. It is the \emph{specificity}, not the
range, that the test rejects.

Separately, the question of whether the reaction module encodes genuine
transcriptional coupling is addressed by comparing the model's counterfactual gene--gene responses against
measured CRISPRa responses \citep{norman2019perturbseq}.
\textbf{This is an out-of-context probe and is treated as such}: Norman et al.\
profiled non-spatial K562 cells, whereas the operator here is fitted to tissue. Fitting is performed on the human breast section so that species and a
proliferative context are at least shared; a permutation null is reported, as is
the same quantity for every baseline.

\paragraph{This probe is underpowered and is treated as inconclusive.} Only
\PSNPerturb{} of Norman's single-gene perturbations fall inside the spatial
model's gene vocabulary (\PSNShared{} genes are shared overall): HVG selection on
a breast-cancer section and a CRISPRa screen in K562 agree on very few targets.
With $n=\PSNPerturb$ the test cannot separate absence of effect from absence of
power. The observed value ($\rho = \PSRho$ against a null of \PSNull, Wilcoxon
$p = \PSWilcoxonP$) is consistent with zero and supports no conclusion in either direction. It is reported because the experiment was pre-specified, not
because it settled anything; a properly powered version would need a screen whose
targets were chosen to overlap the spatial panel.
